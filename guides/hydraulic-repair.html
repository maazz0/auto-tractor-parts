A weak hydraulic lift stops your work immediately. Whether you're sowing wheat, lifting a trailer, or operating a rotavator — the moment the three-point hitch won't rise, you're losing Rs. 15,000–30,000 per day in delayed fieldwork.

At our Vehari workshop, we repair 8–12 hydraulic systems weekly. This guide shows you the 5-step diagnosis that saves you from being overcharged, and the real cost breakdown for every repair.

The 5-Step Diagnosis (Do This First)

1

Check Hydraulic Oil Level

Most common cause. Dipstick should show level between MIN and MAX. Low oil = air in system = jerky, weak lift. Top up with UTTO (Universal Tractor Transmission Oil) — not engine oil.

2

Inspect Oil Color & Smell

Milky oil = water contamination (seal failure). Burnt smell = overheated pump. Dark/black = overdue oil change. All three require oil flush before diagnosis continues.

3

Listen to Pump Sound

High-pitched whining = pump cavitating (low oil or blocked filter). Grinding noise = pump bearing failure. No sound at all = PTO shaft or pump drive failure.

4

Check Lift Under Load

Empty lift works but fails with 500kg+ load = worn pump vanes or internal leakage. Lift rises but drops on its own = control valve or lift cylinder seals.

5

Pressure Test (Workshop Required)

We connect a pressure gauge to the test port. Healthy pump: 200–225 bar. Weak pump: below 180 bar. This confirms diagnosis before any part replacement.

When to Repair vs Replace the Pump

✅ Repair the Pump If:

  • Pump housing is intact (no cracks)
  • Issue is worn vanes, seals, or bearings (common)
  • Repair cost is under 50% of new pump price
  • Pump is under 8 years old

🔄 Replace the Pump If:

  • Housing is cracked or scored
  • Internal gear teeth are damaged
  • Multiple repairs in past 2 years
  • Pump is older than 10 years (rebuild rarely lasts)

⚠️ Avoid "Cheap Copy" Pumps

Chinese-copy hydraulic pumps sell for Rs. 12,000–18,000 (half the OEM price). We've installed them for customers who insisted — average lifespan: 8–14 months. Genuine or Italian OEM pumps last 6–8 years. The "savings" cost you 4x more in the long run.

Prevention: 4 Habits That Double Pump Life

  1. Never run low on oil. Even 30 seconds of dry running destroys pump vanes. Check weekly.
  2. Change oil every 1,000 hours (or yearly). UTTO oil breaks down under heat and loses anti-wear additives.
  3. Replace filter every oil change. A clogged filter starves the pump — the #1 cause of premature failure.
  4. Don't lift loads beyond capacity. MF 285 = 1,600kg max, MF 385 = 2,200kg max. Overloading overheats oil.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long does a hydraulic pump repair take?
A: Rebuild (repair): 4–6 hours, usually same-day. New pump replacement: 2–3 hours. We keep common pumps in stock — no waiting for parts on MF 240/260/285/385.
Q: Can I use engine oil instead of UTTO in hydraulics?
A: Absolutely not. Engine oil lacks the anti-wear additives for hydraulic pumps and will destroy seals within 200 hours. UTTO is specifically designed for tractor transmissions and hydraulics. Rs. 4,000 oil saves Rs. 50,000 pump.
Q: My lift raises slowly but eventually reaches full height. Is this a problem?
A: Yes. Slow lift = early pump wear. Catch it now and a Rs. 5,000 seal kit often fixes it. Wait until it fails completely and you'll need a Rs. 30,000+ pump rebuild. Book a pressure test.
Q: Why does my lift drop when tractor is off?
A: Leaking lift cylinder seals or faulty control valve check valve. Both are repairable — seal kit is Rs. 2,400–3,200 plus Rs. 1,500 labor. Not an emergency but should be fixed before sowing season.
Q: Do you repair New Holland 640S hydraulics?
A: Yes, we specialize in NH 480, 640, 640S hydraulics. We stock genuine Al Ghazi/New Holland pumps, seals, and control valves. Diagnosis time: same day. Repair: 1–2 days.
